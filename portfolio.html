Health and Sensor Analytics

Phone sensors turned into health and activity measures · 3 projects

  1. A phone in a pocket can tell walking, sitting, rowing and jumping apart: a decision tree named the right activity for 99% of two-second slices.

    Recorded our own phone accelerometer data while walking, sitting, rowing and jumping (about 117,000 readings), then trained a classifier to name the activity from any two-second slice. Team project with Alan Tang and Brian Evans, 2023.

    • Rotated every reading into a phone-independent frame, so it didn’t matter how the phone sat in a pocket
    • Engineered 40 features per window, including dominant frequency, peak count and entropy
    • A depth-3 decision tree scored 99.3% in 10-fold cross-validation and 98.1% on stretches of each recording it never saw
    • All data came from one person and one phone, so these are best-case numbers

    Revisited in 2026

    • Fixed feature code that returned uneven arrays and broke the feature table
    • Lengthened windows from 0.2 to 2 seconds, skipped windows that straddle two activities, and added the held-out test

    Tools

    • Python
    • NumPy
    • SciPy
    • scikit-learn
    • Matplotlib

  2. A fingertip over a phone camera, filtered and peak-counted: 71 heartbeats and 7 breaths per minute.

    Estimated heart rate and breathing rate from about a minute of photoplethysmography (PPG) readings captured with a phone camera, the same idea smartwatches use. Group assignment, 2023.

    • Separated the heartbeat from breathing and baseline drift with a high-pass Butterworth filter, then counted peaks: 71 beats per minute
    • Removed the heartbeat with a notch filter and smoothed with a low-pass filter to recover breathing: 7.2 breaths per minute
    • Checked both rates against the strongest frequencies in the signal’s spectrum (66 beats and 8.1 breaths per minute)

    Revisited in 2026

    • The filters now use the camera’s measured sampling rate (about 5.5 Hz); the original assumed 50 to 100 Hz
    • The notch filter now targets the heart rate (about 1.2 Hz) instead of about 0.02 Hz

    Tools

    • Python
    • NumPy
    • SciPy
    • Matplotlib
    • Jupyter

  3. A phone streams its accelerometer to a live web dashboard that counts your steps as you walk.

    A phone running the Sensor Logger app streams accelerometer readings over Wi-Fi to a small Python server, and a dashboard that refreshes every second plots the signal and keeps a running step count. Team project, 2023: Alan Tang led the live data and algorithms, Brian Evans worked on algorithms and plotting, and I worked on plotting.

    • Combined the three axes into one orientation-independent signal and smoothed it with a 2.5 Hz low-pass filter
    • Counted a step at each peak that stands out by 0.8 standard deviations, at least 0.3 seconds apart
    • Within about 10% on the course’s pocket test logs; on the wrist, arm swing makes it over-count
    • Demoed live in class with the phone in a teammate’s pocket

    Revisited in 2026

    • Fixed a crash whenever data arrived, and stopped counting troughs as steps, which had roughly doubled the count
    • Added a running total that doesn’t double-count steps as the 10-second plot scrolls

    Tools

    • Python
    • Dash
    • Plotly
    • Flask
    • SciPy

Data Analysis and Statistics

Clustering and regression on large public datasets · 2 projects

  1. Counties that volunteer more also have more friendships across income lines (r = 0.39). In Massachusetts, Hampshire and Franklin lead.

    Explored the Social Capital Atlas (Chetty et al., 2022): county data on volunteering, civic life and economic connectedness, the friendships between low- and high-income people that most predict upward mobility. Individual project, 2022.

    • Clustered 3,089 counties with k-means, choosing the number of groups by silhouette score: about 910 high-engagement counties volunteer at nearly twice the rate of the rest (11.4% vs. 6.3%)
    • In Massachusetts, Hampshire and Franklin counties lead in volunteering, followed by Dukes; Suffolk, Essex and Hampden are among the lowest
    • Volunteering and civic organizations together explain about 22% of the county-to-county differences in economic connectedness: a correlation, not causation

    Revisited in 2026

    • Removed an API key from the original script, and refit the regression with an intercept
    • Chose the number of clusters by silhouette score instead of fixing it at 5

    Tools

    • Python
    • pandas
    • scikit-learn
    • statsmodels
    • seaborn

  2. Across 83,439 survey responses, the median starting salary was about $20,000, and age, experience and company size explained little of the rest.

    Used the 2021 Stack Overflow Developer Survey (83,439 responses) to ask what drives a developer’s pay, especially in the first year. Team project with Eli Nottonson, 2022.

    • The median starting salary is about $20,000, and about 40% of the 4,138 early-career respondents earn under $15,000; many of them work outside the U.S.
    • Age, years coding and company size are tied to salary but explain little of it (test R² ≈ 0.07)
    • After those controls the gender gap was small and not significant, but women were under 5% of the sample, so that’s weak evidence either way
    • Country is the obvious missing variable, and the natural next step

    Revisited in 2026

    • Fixed an experience filter that compared text and dropped everyone with less than a year
    • Treated years coding as a number instead of about 50 categories, and compared the model with a predict-the-average baseline

    Tools

    • R
    • ggplot2
    • Linear and stepwise regression

Data Visualization and GIS

Dashboards and maps that answer a question at a glance · 2 projects

  1. Tableau views on retail sales: which categories earn the most, which products sell best, and which cities lose money.

    Built a set of Tableau views on a European retail store’s orders to answer business questions: which categories earn the most, which products sell best, and which cities lose money.

    • Created a calculated profit margin field to rank cities by profit-to-sales ratio
    • Charted profit by category and found the top product sub-categories and best-selling products
    • Tracked yearly profit for each country from 2011 to 2014

    Tools

    • Tableau Desktop
    • Calculated fields
    • Dashboards

  2. How much corn and soybean land sits inside 52 Great Lakes and Upper Mississippi watersheds, measured from USDA satellite crop maps.

    Measured how much land was planted with corn and soybeans inside 52 watersheds in the Great Lakes and Upper Mississippi basins, using USDA satellite crop maps. In 2009 the watersheds held about 20,600 km² of corn and 9,200 km² of soybeans.

    • Loaded watershed boundary shapefiles and reprojected them to an equal-area projection
    • Summed the exact pixel area of each crop inside every watershed boundary
    • Wrote the calculation to loop over the years 2009 to 2020; the results shown here are for 2009

    Tools

    • R
    • sf
    • raster
    • exactextractr
    • USDA Cropland Data Layer
